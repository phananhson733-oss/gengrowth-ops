# LinkedIn（English, overseas founder/operator）

AI didn't turn one person into a company. If anything, it's turning companies into networks.

My first bet with GenGrowth was the opposite: that AI could turn it into a growth department that ran itself. This spring I built exactly that — one orchestrator agent coordinating SEO, GEO, and five social agents. On paper it was complete. In practice it could do everything except produce real value. What actually moved the business was tearing it down to a minimal loop with a human in it.

That failure pointed at something bigger than my design. AI widens what any individual can reach, but it doesn't erase the edge of their expertise — and that edge is closer than it looks. In a 2025 METR randomized trial, experienced developers working in codebases they already knew expected AI to make them 24% faster; they came out 19% slower, and still believed they'd been sped up. AI covers the generic playbook and quietly misses the non-obvious detail that decides whether the work lands.

So I don't think the future is "one person does everything." Malone and Laubacher predicted the real shape in HBR back in 1998: as coordination cost falls, the basic unit of business shifts from the firm back to the individual — specialists who assemble around an outcome, much like a film crew, then move on.

The caveat matters. A network of specialists isn't automatically more efficient than a company. It works only when someone owns the outcome, the interfaces between nodes are clean, and context lives in a shared system. Get that wrong and you don't get a network — you get noise.

We're now testing this directly. GenGrowth moved away from making one AI system run the whole growth loop on its own; for now, we're building co-publishing partnerships with products that already show early demand: we hold the growth variable, the partner holds the product, AI amplifies each side. Not one person doing everything — different specialists collaborating at lower cost.

The scarcest skill in the AI era may not be doing everything. It's knowing which part to own, which to hand to AI, and which to hand to someone who knows more than you.

Documenting the pivot as we go.

## LinkedIn — 中文对照（供查验，不发布）

AI 没把一个人变成一家公司。要说有什么变化，是它在把公司变成一张网。

我最初对 GenGrowth 的押注恰恰相反：以为 AI 能把它做成一个"能自运转的增长部门"。今年春天我真就搭了这么一套——一个总控 agent 协调 SEO、GEO 和五个社媒 agent。纸面上很完整，实际上除了产生真正的价值，它什么都能做。真正让业务动起来的，是我把它拆回一个带人工的最小闭环。

那次失败指向的，是比"我设计得不好"更大的事。AI 拓宽了每个人能触及的范围，却没有抹掉他专业的边界——而这道边界比看上去近。2025 年 METR 的随机对照实验里，资深开发者在自己熟悉的代码库上，原以为 AI 能让他们快 24%，结果慢了 19%，还以为自己变快了。AI 覆盖通用套路，却悄悄漏掉那个决定成败的非常规细节。

所以我不认为未来是"一个人什么都干"。马隆和劳巴赫早在 1998 年就在《哈佛商业评论》预言了真正的形态：当协调成本下降，商业的基本单元会从公司回到个人——一群专业个体围绕一个结果聚起来，像剧组一样，完事再散。

但有个前提很重要。一张专业个体的网，并不天然比一家公司更高效。只有当有人对结果负责、节点之间接口清晰、上下文沉淀在一个共享系统里，它才成立。搞砸这几条，你得到的不是网络，而是噪声。

我们正在直接验证这件事。GenGrowth 不再想让一套 AI 系统自己跑完整条增长链；眼下先和"已经显现早期需求"的产品做合作发行：我们守住增长这个变量，合作方守住产品，AI 放大各自。不是一个人包办一切，而是不同专业的人以更低的成本协作。

AI 时代最稀缺的本事，也许不是什么都会干，而是拎得清：哪一块自己扛、哪一块交给 AI、哪一块交给一个比你更懂的人。
