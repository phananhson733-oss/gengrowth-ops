# Off the Ice: What Is Albert Actually Lying About?

**Meta description:** A concussion wipes hockey captain Lucas's memory of hating Albert — so Albert tells him they were secretly dating. Here's what the free ShortsBinge episodes show.

---

[Off the Ice](https://shortsbinge.com/drama/off-the-ice-omwkkjk24kut/) sets up its premise in one sentence and then spends nine free episodes complicating it: a concussion wipes college hockey captain Lucas Carter's memory of his rivalry with transfer student Albert Clay, and Albert fills the gap with a lie — that the two of them have been secret lovers all along. Lucas still has a girlfriend. He still thinks he's straight. He still can't explain why none of that adds up anymore. [Episode 1](https://shortsbinge.com/drama/off-the-ice-omwkkjk24kut/episode/1/) doesn't ease into any of this — more on that below.

## Nine of 50 Episodes Are Free, and They Don't Start Where You'd Expect

Most of these guides open by describing episode 1 as the setup. This one doesn't work that way. The first episode is a flash-forward — an intimate, already-established-relationship scene between Lucas and Albert, with no dialogue to explain how they got there. Episode 2 is the actual beginning: a hostile locker-room exchange, a "fuck off" thrown across the room, real rivalry with no romance in sight yet. If you watch in order, expect whiplash on purpose — the show is telling you where this ends before it tells you how.

The free run bookends that opening with a second gut-punch. Episode 9 closes on a woman in a black dress grabbing a man's arm on a sunlit campus street, and the only line either of them gets is his stammered "I... uh..." before it cuts. Nothing about who she is or what she's asking is spelled out on screen — but nine episodes in, there's only one relationship left that a confrontation like that could be about.

## The Lie Only Works Because Lucas Can't Remember the Truth

The mechanic here is specific in a way worth spelling out: this isn't amnesia as a vague plot device, it's amnesia as the exact condition that makes Albert's lie survivable. Lucas has no memory to contradict Albert with — no fight to point to, no reason to doubt him, nothing except a vague unease that something isn't adding up. That's a narrower premise than most memory-loss dramas attempt, and it means the tension isn't "will Lucas find out" so much as "what does Lucas do with feelings he has no memory of choosing."

## The Team Still Treats Him Like Their Captain

Whatever the concussion changed for Lucas privately, it hasn't cost him the locker room. Episode 3 catches a teammate grinning at him over a pass — "Hell of a pass, captain!" — the kind of easy, earned respect that doesn't get handed to someone people have doubts about. That matters for the amnesia premise specifically: Lucas isn't rebuilding his identity from zero in front of a team that's watching him warily. Everyone around him still sees the captain they always had. He's the only one who isn't sure that's still accurate.

The ice itself isn't just a backdrop either. Episode 5 cuts to real game footage — a hard check, a player down against the boards, the kind of hit that's exactly how someone in this sport ends up with a concussion in the first place. The show doesn't need a line of dialogue to remind you what's structurally at stake here: the sport that defines Lucas is also the one that could do this to him again.

## Albert Isn't Lying Just to Get the Guy

ShortsBinge's own synopsis is careful not to frame Albert as a simple schemer — it promises "the truth behind Albert's deception" as something the season builds toward, and files the drama under a Redemption story beat alongside Enemies to Lovers. That combination suggests Albert's lie isn't opportunism so much as leftover damage from whatever the actual rivalry was about before the concussion — which reframes the fake relationship as Albert trying to fix something he broke, badly, in the worst possible way.

## Jessica Doesn't Disappear Just Because the Plot Needs Her To

One detail a lot of amnesia-romance loglines would drop: Lucas still has a girlfriend, Jessica, for at least part of this. She's named directly in the official synopsis, not written out to simplify the pitch. That means the free episodes aren't just building an enemies-to-lovers arc, they're building one with a real person on the other side of the eventual fallout — which tracks with the Redemption tag doing double duty here. That confrontation on the sunlit street closing out episode 9 reads like the moment this stops being avoidable for everyone involved, Jessica included.
