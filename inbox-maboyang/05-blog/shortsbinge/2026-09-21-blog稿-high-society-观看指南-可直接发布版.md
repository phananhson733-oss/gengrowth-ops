# High Society: Can a Fake Sister Fall for the Duke?

**Meta description:** High Society follows Adele posing as a Duke's fake sister to dodge his arranged marriage — until the one rule between them breaks. Free guide.

---

[High Society](https://shortsbinge.com/drama/high-society-yk4yzs3e3nsm/) runs on a premise that sounds simple until you sit with it: a girl with no name agrees to pretend to be a Duke's long-lost sister so he can dodge a marriage he doesn't want. The catch is built into the lie itself — siblings, even fake ones, aren't allowed to fall for each other. Everything interesting about this drama comes from watching that one rule strain under pressure it was never built to survive.

## Adele: The Girl Who Had to Invent Herself to Survive

Adele's defining trait isn't beauty or charm, it's the fact that she has nothing to fall back on — no last name, no family, no home. That's not a throwaway detail; it's the entire reason she's willing to walk into a Duke's household and lie about who she is. A heroine with a safety net turns down a deal this dangerous. Adele doesn't have one, so the [strong-willed](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/identities/strong-willed/) resolve the story keeps highlighting isn't framed as a personality quirk — it's what happens to someone who has had to talk her way into every room she's ever been allowed in.

That also reframes the "fake sister" premise itself: for Adele, this isn't a whim, it's the best offer she's ever had, which means she has more to lose than Cesare does if the [hidden identity](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/hidden-identity/) at the center of the deal ever slips.

## Cesare Buonaparte and the Della Valle Family He's Trying to Escape

Cesare Buonaparte is the Duke of the island nation of Santenare, and by the story's own telling, the most feared man on it. Feared doesn't mean free, though — the entire scheme with Adele exists because Cesare is being pushed into a marriage to Lucrezia Della Valle, a wealthy heiress the story frames as the arranged match he's maneuvering to avoid. According to ReelShort's own character materials, Lucrezia doubles as the drama's central rival once Adele enters the picture, and her brother Esdra Della Valle — heir to the same influential family — is part of the same social web Adele has to navigate as she's pulled deeper into high society. That's the real shape of the [love triangle](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/love-triangle/) tag ShortsBinge applies here: it's not just Adele and Cesare with a third party circling, it's an entire family with its own stake in making sure the marriage happens.

One more character worth knowing before you start: ReelShort's broader character guide also names Nino, the boss running the slum district Adele comes from, as the person who controlled her fate before the Duke's offer gave her a way out. ShortsBinge's own credited cast for the series lists four leads — Maria Barseghian as Adele, Chase Mattson as Cesare, Andrew Tong as Esdra, and Celine Planata as Lucrezia — so Nino appears to be a smaller role that didn't make that shorter list, but it's worth knowing he exists as the stakes Adele is running from, not just what she's running toward.

## Love-Hate, Taboo: Why "Just Pretend" Was Never Going to Hold

ShortsBinge tags the emotional core of High Society as both [love-hate](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/love-hate/) and [taboo](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/taboo/), and together those two tags explain why the premise doesn't stay contained. Love-hate means the conflict between Adele and Cesare doesn't resolve and stay resolved — it's built to keep flipping, so every scene where they get along is followed by one where the lie, the class gap, or the arranged marriage forces them back apart. Taboo means the obstacle isn't just external circumstance, it's the rule they agreed to themselves: act like siblings, no matter what either of them starts to feel. That's a harder rule to break than an outside objection would be, because breaking it means admitting the entire scheme was a lie from the start — not just to Cesare's family, but to each other.

## From a 13-Million-View Webcomic to Vertical Video

High Society isn't an original ReelShort concept — it's adapted from a webcomic by the artist Gyeonu, published through Manta Comics and also hosted on WEBTOON, where it has racked up over 13.1 million views and more than 142,000 subscribers with new chapters releasing weekly. That's a real, sizable readership that existed before ShortsBinge or ReelShort ever optioned it, which puts High Society in a different category from a premise built from scratch for short-form video.

It's worth noting the source webcomic has drawn some mixed critical attention of its own: a Comics Beat review praised Adele's characterization and the story's pacing, but also flagged uncomfortable execution choices around how Adele's early disguise and appearance are handled before her transformation into a society debutante. That's a criticism of the original webcomic specifically — whether the short-drama adaptation handles that same material the same way isn't something ShortsBinge's own materials confirm either way, so it's a reasonable thing to watch for rather than a settled verdict on the show itself.

## Why One Critic Says It Works Despite the Fake-Sibling Discomfort

The drama adaptation has its own independent critical reception, separate from the webcomic. Entertainment site Fangirlish gave it 4.5 out of 5 stars, with reviewer Erin E. Gross calling it "entertaining, snarky, & different in the best way possible" and praising the chemistry between leads Chase Mattson and Maria Barseghian as a big part of why it works. Notably, she's upfront that the premise didn't sit easily with her at first — she admits to "watching through my fingers" during some of the early fake-sibling scenes — before the show won her over enough that she rewatched it immediately after finishing.

That's a more useful signal than a simple thumbs-up: a critic who found the setup genuinely uncomfortable at first, and was still persuaded by the execution, says more about whether the show delivers than a review with no reservations at all.
