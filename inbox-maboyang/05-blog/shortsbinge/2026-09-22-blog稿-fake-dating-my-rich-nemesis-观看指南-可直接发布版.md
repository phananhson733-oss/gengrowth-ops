[Fake Dating My Rich Nemesis](https://shortsbinge.com/drama/fake-dating-my-rich-nemesis-6f4x2xa4vnco/) starts from a setup built for exactly one purpose: get back at an ex. Tessa Sinclair returns to North Lake High and ends up striking a deal with Ronan Beaumont, the boy she's clashed with since childhood — they'll pretend to date, just long enough to make their respective exes jealous. The premise is simple. What makes the show work is how badly that plan goes according to schedule.

## Two Different Reasons to Say Yes to the Same Bad Idea

Tessa and Ronan agree to the same scheme, but they're not doing it for the same reason, and that gap is worth paying attention to. Tessa's sharp tongue and refusal to back down read as generic "spunky heroine" material until you clock where it actually comes from: she's spent years already knowing exactly how Ronan thinks, so none of her bravado toward him is a bluff — it's armor built specifically for him, not for the world in general. Ronan's motive runs the other way. He's the school's untouchable rich [heir](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/identities/childhood-sweetheart/), which sounds like an advantage until you notice how much of his real life he's hiding behind it. The credited cast includes both his mother, Helen Beaumont, and his father, and outside coverage of the series specifically describes his arc as growing "from privileged heir to standing up against toxic parenting" — meaning the fake relationship isn't just about an ex for him, it's the first low-stakes place he gets to practice being someone other than the Beaumont name.

Put together: she's protecting herself from a person she already understands too well, and he's using the deal to briefly step outside a role he didn't choose. That's a more interesting engine than "two people who happen to hate each other," because it means their reasons for staying in the lie don't run in parallel — they're solving two completely different problems with the same fake couple.

## Jessie and Beckett: The Exes Who Keep Losing and Keep Coming Back

Every fake-dating plot needs someone to perform for, and here that job falls to Jessie and Beckett Hasting, the pair Tessa and Ronan are trying to make jealous. Coverage from the vertical-drama site I Love Verticals sums the two of them up as having "Team Rocket energy" — a duo who keep scheming, keep losing, and keep showing up for more anyway, played more for comic escalation than genuine menace. That's a deliberate tonal choice worth knowing before you start: the obstacle driving the plot isn't a credible threat to root against, it's a recurring joke that also happens to keep raising the stakes. If you're expecting a serious rival who might actually win Ronan or Tessa back, recalibrate — the real tension in this show isn't "will the exes succeed," it's what Tessa and Ronan discover about each other while performing for an audience that was never that dangerous to begin with.

## Milo Is Rooting for Them Before They Are

One recurring character worth knowing about going in: Milo, a friend in Ronan's orbit who reviewers describe as functioning like the audience's own stand-in — teasing Ronan about Tessa while quietly nudging him toward her the whole time. It's a small structural choice that does real work: instead of the audience being the only ones who can see where this is headed, there's a character inside the story who's already placing bets on it too. When a supporting character is written to notice the chemistry before the leads will admit to it, that's usually a sign the writers trust the central relationship enough to let someone comment on it in real time rather than saving all the reveals for the leads themselves.
