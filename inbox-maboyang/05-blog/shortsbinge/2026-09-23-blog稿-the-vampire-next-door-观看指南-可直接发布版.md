## Victoria Isn't Just "a Vampire" — She's 247 Years Old and Out for Revenge

ShortsBinge's synopsis keeps Victoria vague: a vampire, a dangerous situation, lives at stake. The actual backstory, confirmed through the film's own listings, is much more specific — Victoria was turned at sixteen and is now 247 years old, and she's hunting the pack of vampires responsible for killing her lover back in 1890. That's a genuine revenge-thriller engine sitting underneath what the [neighbors](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/neighbors/) framing makes sound like a cute meet-cute setup. Cameron isn't just an awkward guy with a crush who gets swept up in something — he's a [wallflower](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/identities/wallflower/) dropped into a 130-year-old blood feud he has no context for.

## The Blackmail, the Vampire Hunt, and a Terrible Wingman Plan

The free episodes set up more mechanical detail than ShortsBinge's synopsis lets on. Cameron first notices Victoria through his own window — and she promptly uses that against him, threatening to tell his parents he's been peeping unless he agrees to drive her around. It's on one of those drives that he witnesses her attacking someone and puts together what she actually is. His reaction isn't to run: relieved that her hit list is limited to other vampires rather than ordinary people, he agrees to help with her revenge mission instead. That's a very different way into a "dangerous adventure" than a vague synopsis line suggests — Cameron isn't swept up by accident, he opts in once he's decided the danger doesn't extend to him.

Running alongside the vampire plot is a much smaller, much more embarrassing one: Cameron's crush on his longtime friend Diane. When Victoria catches him scheming to set Diane up on a date with a guy named Martin, purely so he can manage the situation from the sidelines, she tells him bluntly to just say what he feels instead. He doesn't take the advice cleanly — he ends up coaching Martin over the phone in real time to help him impress Diane on their date, a plan that collapses the moment Diane realizes what's happening. Meanwhile, watching Victoria and Diane grow closer to each other adds a layer of confusion to a plan that was already going sideways. None of that resolves in the free run, but it tells you the film is working two very different registers at once — a centuries-old blood feud and a teenager's humiliatingly bad wingman scheme — which lines up with Taliesin's complaint about tonal whiplash even as it explains what the movie is actually going for.

## Where This Fits in the "My Neighbor Is a Vampire" Genre

The 80s-throwback label the studio applies to this isn't just marketing shorthand — there's a real lineage here. Fright Night (1985) is generally credited with reviving vampire horror-comedy for that decade, The Lost Boys (1987) turned vampirism into a cool teenage rebellion fantasy, and Once Bitten (1985) played it as a dating problem. The closest direct ancestor to this specific premise, though, is My Best Friend Is a Vampire (1987) — a teen comedy built on almost the exact same hook: an ordinary kid already struggling with girls, school, and parents discovers someone close to him is a vampire, and has to handle it on top of everything else already going wrong in his life. Swap "best friend" for "next-door neighbor" and you have The Vampire Next Door's whole premise. That's not a coincidence so much as a genre studio deliberately writing into a 40-year-old comedy formula rather than inventing a new one — which is worth knowing if you want to judge the film on the terms it's actually working in, rather than as a failed attempt at straight horror.

## This Is a 2024 Movie, Not a ReelShort Original

Here's the part worth knowing before you commit any time to this: "The Vampire Next Door" already exists as an independent film, directed and written by Sean King, starring Alex Matthews as Cameron, Jessica Ferguson as Victoria, and Bella Chadwick as Diane. It's been available on Prime Video, Pluto TV, Apple TV, Tubi, and other platforms since its January 2024 release — under the exact same title, with the exact same plot description ReelShort uses. The film is pitched by its own studio as an "80s-style horror comedy," which is a useful frame ShortsBinge's dramatic synopsis doesn't give you: this is meant to be a throwback genre pastiche, not a serious modern thriller, and that context changes what "dangerous situation" and "life-and-death adventure" should actually signal to you as a viewer.

Sean King works as a filmmaking team with his son Taylor King (who also has an on-screen role here, as a character named Lenny), operating under their own studio, New Zealand Son Films, founded in 2015. Together they've written, directed, and produced 19 features and TV series distributed in 160 countries across more than 30 platforms, including Amazon Prime, Apple TV, Tubi, Flix, and Roku. If the low-budget genre-throwback style is what you're after, their other named titles include Zombie Repellent, Alien Weekend, and Super Hot. ## Two Very Different Verdicts on the Same Story

The film carries a real critical record, and it's mixed. On IMDb, it sits at 4.8 out of 10 from over 1,100 ratings — not a tiny sample, and not a flattering number either. Horror blogger Taliesin, reviewing it independently, scored it 3.5 out of 10 and diagnosed the core problem directly: the film "can't make up its mind" whether it's a vampire story, a romance, a comedy, or a high-school movie, and doesn't fully commit to any of them. The vampire lore is thin, the hunts are over quickly, and Cameron accepting Victoria's vampirism reads as unearned rather than dramatic. The one consistent point of praise, across multiple reviews, is Alex Matthews's performance as Cameron — described as "genuinely engaging" even by reviewers who otherwise found the film unfocused.

None of that means you won't enjoy the free episodes — plenty of low-budget horror-comedy has an audience precisely because it doesn't take itself too seriously — but it does mean the [self-growth](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/self-growth/) arc ShortsBinge tags this with is riding on a script that even sympathetic reviewers found undercooked. Go in with those expectations set correctly rather than assuming ShortsBinge's dramatic framing reflects the finished product's actual tone.

For a sense of how differently two honest viewers can land on the same movie, put Taliesin's 3.5 next to a Letterboxd review that gave it an 8 and called it "so bad it's good," comparing Victoria to Jennifer Check from Jennifer's Body and singling out Cameron's dorky charm — his Star Wars figures, his Marty-and-Doc Back to the Future toys — as part of what made the whole thing endearing rather than embarrassing. That review has racked up 183 likes, which suggests it's not a lone outlier take. The gap between "this doesn't know what it wants to be" and "the mess is the whole appeal" is really a question of what you're in the mood for, not a contradiction in the reporting.
