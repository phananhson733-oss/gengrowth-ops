# The Great and Powerful Genie: The Full Story, Ruby Hiller, and How It Ends

**Meta description:** Elijah Baran grants a fisherman's wishes, then marries his granddaughter for five years to win his freedom. The full story of The Great and Powerful Genie, the characters, and the ending explained.

---

In 1966, a young man named Steven Parrish went fishing to feed his family and pulled up a magic lamp instead. Decades later he's one of the richest men in the city, and on his deathbed he makes one last wish: to set the genie free. The condition is that Elijah Baran must marry his granddaughter, Christine, and take care of her with all his heart for five years.

## What Kind of Genie Is Elijah?

Elijah Baran has spent thousands of years granting wishes from inside a lamp. According to [ReelShort's fandom guide to his story](https://www.reelshort.com/fandom/elijah-baran-genie-full-story-01-1-4f8c5d112d00/ "nofollow"), he was once a living being himself, which is why he understands the desperation behind the wishes he hears. He grants them without complaint, but his power has limits, and the masters who tried to bend his rules didn't come out of it well.

What he wanted most was the one thing he couldn't grant himself: freedom. Many of his masters were grateful, some even treated him like a god, but none of them was willing to give up a wish to release him. Steven Parrish was the first.

## The Wish Written into a Will

Steven's first wishes were about money, comfort and his family. They made him the founder of Parrish Enterprises. His last wish, though, was for Elijah's freedom, with a catch designed to protect his granddaughter.

The fandom coverage explains how the condition works. Steven writes the wish into his will, and Christine reads it at his funeral. If she refuses to marry Elijah, all of Steven's assets go to the shareholders. So Christine marries a stranger to keep her inheritance, and Elijah takes human form to fulfill the last wish of the man who freed him.

That setup explains Christine's hostility from the first day. She didn't choose this marriage. What the story asks is whether five years of kindness can change her mind.

## Five Years Go By in an Episode

The show doesn't dwell on the marriage itself. By episode 2, the contract is only days from ending, and Christine is as cold as ever. Everything that follows plays out in that countdown, and the free episodes keep returning to one bitter pattern: Elijah does something remarkable, and someone else gets the credit.

It starts with the Hiller deal. Christine needs the Hiller Group on board, so Elijah visits Adrian Hiller, an old friend who once asked the genie for help, and cures him of a deadly disease. Adrian agrees to back the Parrish family. When Elijah tells his wife, she laughs at him, and her mother sends him off to drive her to the salon.

The birthday party is the scene most viewers remember. Elijah comes home with a gift he chose carefully and finds a party where Christine's friends have gathered to mock him. Tony Roth, her ex, walks in with a diamond necklace. Elijah's gift ends up at the bottom of the pool, and he dives into the cold water to get it back, then leaves without a word.

The free run closes on two moments that point toward the ending. When Christine calls him a gold digger, Elijah calmly tells her that her wish will come true in a few days, and she's strangely unsettled to watch him walk out. Then the Hiller deal closes without a hitch, Christine decides Tony must be responsible, and she asks Elijah to drive her to the Maple Hotel to meet him.

## Ruby Hiller: The Character the Free Episodes Barely Show

If you only watch the free run, you'll barely notice Ruby Hiller. According to [ReelShort's character guide](https://www.reelshort.com/fandom/the-great-and-powerful-genie-cast-01-2-4f8c5d112d00/ "nofollow"), she starts the story as little more than Peter Hiller's sister, part of a family that has tried every doctor to save their grandfather, Adrian.

She becomes far more important. After Elijah heals her grandfather, Ruby starts noticing strange things about him. His injuries heal far faster than a human's should. She begins to suspect he's "the Great One" her grandfather has told stories about, and she confirms it at one of the company's spiritual meetings.

Where Christine sees a nobody, Ruby sees exactly who Elijah is, and she treasures him for it. She tells her grandfather how much she loves Elijah, and she isn't shy about telling everyone else. The fandom coverage describes her as classy, modest and respectful, the opposite of Christine in almost every way.

## Tony Roth Is More Than a Jealous Ex

Tony looks like a standard rival in the early episodes, but the character guide fills in more. He's a former football star proud of his Super Bowl win, Christine's college boyfriend, and a golf buddy of Peter Hiller.

His role grows darker as the story goes on. He takes credit for the Hiller deal that Elijah secured. Later episodes also show him toying with Elijah's magic lamp, which leads to him being banished. He's the clearest example of the show's moral logic: the people who try to use the genie's power for themselves don't get away with it.

## How It Ends (Spoilers)

*Skip this section if you want to watch the ending for yourself.*

According to ReelShort's fandom guide, Elijah doesn't leave because the contract runs out. He leaves because he's exhausted. Had Christine treated him with even basic respect, he would have stayed married to her after winning his freedom. Instead, she keeps listening to her mother and to Tony, and Elijah finally pushes for a divorce. Christine signs in anger, convinced that he'll regret it.

The truth comes out at a gathering of the most powerful families. Through the deal between the Hillers and the Parrish family, Christine finally learns who her ex-husband is. The Hillers reveal that Elijah is the genie their secret organization has revered, the force behind the Parrish fortune, and the reason their families prospered for generations.

Christine immediately begs him to choose her again and promises never to repeat her mistakes. Elijah steps back, takes Ruby's hand, and walks away. The character guide adds one gentler note: he may heal Christine one last time, but only because caring isn't the same as loving.

ShortsBinge tags the series as [All-Too-Late](https://shortsbinge.com/browse/story-beats/all-too-late/), and the ending is the genre at its purest. The woman who lived on the genie's gifts learns what he was only after she's lost him.

## It's Animated, Not Live Action

ReelShort's own FAQ describes the series as an AI-generated animated original, a motion comic made in English. This version has no cast list, because there are no actors. ReelShort files it under its male-audience shows and rates it suitable for all ages.

If you'd rather see the story with real actors, you can. The same story was released two months earlier as a live-action series, [Djinn Under Contract](https://shortsbinge.com/drama/djinn-under-contract-7lgctgzebyzg/), starring Eric Guilmette and Nikki Leigh. In that version the genie is called Eli Baran, Christine is still a Parrish, and the story runs to 67 episodes. ReelShort also lists it under a second title, I Was Married to the Magic Genie but I Lost Him, which explains why clips with actors and a different name turn up when you search for this show.

The animated format suits the material. A genie who heals fatal diseases, a lamp that can banish people and a secret organization that worships a "Great One" would be hard to stage convincingly in a low-budget live-action short.
