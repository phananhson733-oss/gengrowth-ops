## 三、正文（英文底稿）

Elijah Baran is the genie at the center of *The Great and Powerful Genie* — and if you've been following the drama's sudden surge in popularity, you've probably already seen his name everywhere. Here's everything worth knowing about him, from the lamp to the empire he built in the shadows.

### Who is Elijah Baran?

Elijah is a Djinn — bound to a magic lamp, obedient to whoever holds it, and fluent in the kind of riddling wisdom you'd expect from a being who's existed for millennia. He grants wishes because it's his purpose, but he never hands over control of who he really is. Test his limits and you pay for it; a long line of masters found that out the hard way over the centuries.

Then came Steven Parrish.

### The wish that changed everything

Steven was young and broke when he pulled Elijah's lamp out of the water on an ordinary fishing trip. His first wish was simple: wealth, comfort, a future for his family. Decades later, dying, wealthy beyond measure, Steven had one wish left — and he didn't spend it on himself.

He wished Elijah free. The catch: Elijah had to marry Steven's granddaughter, Christine, and stay as her devoted husband for five years, protecting her no matter what.

Elijah agreed without hesitation. He had no idea what he was walking into.

### The five-year contract, and why it nearly broke him

Christine never wanted this marriage, and she made sure Elijah knew it. He took on every role expected of him — running the household, quietly fixing her business problems from behind the scenes, staying loyal while she treated him like hired help in front of her own family.

Two moments define just how far things went:

1. **The Tony Roth storyline.** Christine's ex re-enters her life as the five-year mark approaches, and her resistance to him cracks slowly, publicly, and painfully for Elijah to watch.
2. **The rescue that backfires.** When Elijah steps in to pull Christine out of a dangerous confrontation with Tony, she turns the blame on him instead of thanking him — the moment that finally convinces Elijah he's done.

### The twist: Elijah Baran isn't just a genie

Here's the part that turns *The Great and Powerful Genie* from a domestic drama into something bigger. Once the five-year contract ends, Elijah walks away from the Parrish family — and straight into The Luminaries, a secret alliance of the world's most powerful people that he founded centuries earlier.

Nobody in Christine's circle knows this. So when Christine's own mother publicly humiliates Elijah at a high-society gathering, she has no idea she's insulting the man whose influence built the very prestige her family enjoys. It's the kind of dramatic irony that makes the back half of the story land so hard — the audience knows exactly who Elijah is long before the Parrish family does.

### Ruby Hiller changes the ending

Elijah doesn't walk away from Christine for nothing — Ruby Hiller gives him a reason to. She's the granddaughter of another family Elijah once helped, and everything Christine isn't: modest, sharp, and willing to stand up for Elijah publicly when it matters. Once Ruby is in the picture, Elijah's decision to end things with Christine stops being just an exit — it becomes a choice toward something better.

By the time the truth about Elijah's identity comes out at the Hiller-Parrish gathering, Christine begs him to stay. He takes Ruby's hand instead and walks out, leaving Christine with nothing but the fallout of five years of taking him for granted.

### Meet the rest of the cast

- **Christine Parrish** — Steven's granddaughter and Elijah's reluctant wife, whose arc is built entirely around learning the cost of taking someone for granted too late.
- **Tony Roth** — Christine's ex, a retired athlete coasting on old fame and charm, and the direct foil to Elijah: all visibility, none of the real power.
- **Ruby Hiller** — the woman who actually deserves Elijah, and the one who sees his worth before anyone else does.

### Watch Elijah Baran's full story

If Elijah's story has you hooked, the full episode run is where the payoff lands hardest — every slight against him hits differently once you know what he's capable of.
