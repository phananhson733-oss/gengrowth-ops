## 三、正文（英文，可直接粘贴）

DramaBox and ReelShort are the two biggest names in vertical short dramas, and most comparisons of them are opinion pieces about which catalog is "better." We went to the actual app store listings and real user reviews instead, and found the two apps have more in common than most comparisons admit — including the exact same complaint.

### Who actually owns each app

**DramaBox** is published on the Google Play Store by **STORYMATRIX PTE. LTD.**, registered in Singapore. Support runs through feedback@dramabox.com.

**ReelShort** is published on the Google Play Store by **NewLeaf Publishing**. The app is widely reported — including by Wikipedia and multiple press outlets — to be owned by **Crazy Maple Studio**, which is backed by the Beijing-based digital publisher COL Group. ReelShort launched in August 2022 and has passed 100 million downloads on Google Play alone (4.0 stars, 1.8M+ reviews).

Neither app hides who runs it — both file the required developer disclosures with Google and Apple — but the entity name on the store listing and the company usually quoted in press coverage are not always the same name, which is worth knowing if you go looking for either company yourself.

### How the content actually differs

Both apps run on the same production model: fast-turnaround vertical dramas, one to a few minutes per episode, heavy on romance, revenge, billionaire and CEO storylines, mafia and werewolf tropes.

The difference is closer to release cadence and curation than genre. DramaBox leans into volume — new titles added constantly across a wide romance/revenge/fantasy spread. ReelShort's catalog is smaller per week but more consistently produced, with a run of English-language originals that get heavier marketing pushes (its app listing quotes coverage from the New York Times, Wall Street Journal and Washington Post).

Neither app licenses outside TV or film content. Everything in both catalogs is produced specifically for the format.

### How you pay

Both apps use the same basic structure: the first several episodes of a series are free, and after that you either unlock episodes with coins or subscribe.

DramaBox's Play Store listing shows in-app purchases from **$0.49 to $299.99 per item**. ReelShort does not publicly list an equivalent range on its own store page; third-party app-intelligence trackers estimate a wider **$0.09 to $199.99 per item**, but we could not confirm that figure from ReelShort's own listing, so treat it as a rough indication rather than a quote.

Coin pricing and subscription cost on both apps vary by title, region and active promotion. Any single dollar figure you see quoted for either app — including the "$17–20/week" number that circulates for DramaBox — should be treated as a starting point, not a current price. Check the live checkout screen in the app before you commit.

### The complaint both apps share

This is the part most comparisons skip: pull up the real reviews for each app, and the top complaint is nearly word-for-word identical.

On DramaBox, the recurring theme is subscriptions that are easy to start and hard to find your way out of, with cancellation handled at the App Store or Google Play level rather than inside the app itself.

On ReelShort's own Google Play listing, multiple reviews describe the same pattern in more detail. One reviewer wrote that after the first payment, the app "changes to a website and makes it almost impossible to cancel the subscription," with no cancel option inside the app itself. Another reported being billed four additional times — $146 total — after attempting to cancel, and had to dispute the charges with their card issuer. NewLeaf Publishing's official replies to both reviews point users to the subscription tab in their App Store or Google Play account, not to anything inside the ReelShort app.

Neither pattern makes either app a scam — both are real companies distributing through official app stores, and both direct you to the correct place (your platform's subscription settings, not the developer) to actually cancel. But it means the advice is identical for both apps: **know how to cancel in your Apple or Google account before you subscribe to either one**, because neither app makes it obvious from inside itself.

### So which one should you download

If you want to know this is legitimate before you commit: yes, both are legitimate, officially distributed apps from identifiable companies. That question is covered in more depth in our [short drama app safety guide](/blog/short-drama-app-safety-guide).

Past that, the honest answer is that the choice comes down to how much you value catalog volume versus curation, not which app is "better." If you like having a constant stream of new titles to sample, DramaBox's release pace serves that better. If you'd rather follow a smaller number of more consistently produced English-language originals, ReelShort fits that pattern more.

What doesn't differ enough to matter: safety, general pricing structure, or how painful cancellation is if you're not paying attention. Budget for that regardless of which one you pick.

## 四、FAQ（结构化数据用）

**Is DramaBox or ReelShort a Chinese app?**
Both originate from companies with roots in the Chinese short-drama industry. ReelShort's parent, Crazy Maple Studio, is backed by Beijing-based COL Group. DramaBox's Play Store developer, STORYMATRIX PTE. LTD., is registered in Singapore. Both distribute internationally through the official Apple and Google app stores.

**Is DramaBox cheaper than ReelShort?**
Neither app publishes one fixed price — both use coins and subscriptions that vary by title, region and promotion. Compare the live price shown in each app for the specific series you want to watch rather than relying on a single quoted figure.

**Why is it hard to cancel a DramaBox or ReelShort subscription?**
Both apps bill through your Apple or Google account rather than directly, and neither has a working cancel button inside the app itself. You have to cancel from your phone's subscription settings on the platform you subscribed through.

**Can I use both apps for free?**
Yes — both give you the first several episodes of any series for free, plus ad-supported or reward-coin ways to unlock more without paying, though the pace is slower than subscribing.
