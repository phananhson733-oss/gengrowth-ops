## Why the Wheelchair Twist Works

Most mafia-husband dramas sell power from the first scene: the suit, the bodyguards, the room going quiet when he walks in. This one holds all of that back. For a real stretch of the story, Leo is exactly what Samantha and Hugh assume he is — a guy in a wheelchair with no money and no leverage, easy to dismiss and easier to bully.

That's the trick. Watching Leo get underestimated by people who have no idea what's coming does more work than another scene of him throwing his weight around would. His authority isn't stated, it's revealed piece by piece, and the payoff lands harder because the story made you sit with his powerlessness first.

It also reframes what the mafia-boss trope is usually selling. This isn't a bad-boy fantasy built on danger and control. Leo's defining trait through the coma and the recovery is devotion — to Olivia, to the promise he never got to make. The power is real, but it's not the point of the character. That's a different pitch than most entries in this genre make, and it's worth knowing going in if you came expecting a straightforward power fantasy.
